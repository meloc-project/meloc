# Meloc Manifest

Only if our software is natural and reliable, can we ensure that it is sustainable. Therefore, Meloc argues that every software must follow concepts as basic as these:

## The six principles
### 1. Human
Human language is above machine language. Cognitive ease, above optimization.

### 2. Data over code
Logic is fragile; structure is stable. Declarations are immutable; instructions can change. Prioritizing contracts on imperative code is what really gives stability and security.

### 3. Simplicity
Every new feature is future technical debt. If something is not essential, it may not be necessary. Reducing functionalities ultimately minimizes the load of decisions and points of failure.

### 4. Transparency
Everything should be clear and visible. Every rule and behavior should be traceable until its definition. You should be able to audit software just by reading it, not running countless, blind tests.

### 5. Security
When limits are declared from the beginning, no patches need to be applied afterwards. An explicit, though noisy, failure is to anticipate the problem; a muted error only prevents the issue from being identified in time.

### 6. Universality
Software must not depend on a specific language or platform. You should be able to execute it on any valid environment.

## Who this is for
Meloc **is not** for those who do not want to change their processes, nor for teams accustomed to complex business patterns.

Meloc **is** for anyone who wants to build tools, prototypes, games, or interfaces without technical debt, and believes that software should serve us, not lower us to their level.

**If you are tired of fighting against systems that should make your life easier, you are in the right place.**
